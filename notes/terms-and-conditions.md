## Most important insights

1. **Participation itself accepts the terms (§1).** Creating an account, registering, or using the platform counts as acceptance. You must also follow the official competition rules, organizer instructions, and venue regulations. Reading this document alone does not establish every competition requirement.
2. **Your independently created work stays yours (§6).** Original team solutions and code remain their authors' intellectual property, subject to third-party rights and applicable law. The terms do not transfer ownership to the organizer. Organizer use beyond competition administration requires an appropriate legal basis and, where necessary, authorization.
3. **Ownership does not give permission to publish all competition content (§§2, 6).** Organizer tasks, materials, platform code, and other protected content have separate restrictions on copying, modification, distribution, and publication. Other teams' solutions, flags, and confidential materials cannot be accessed or shared without authorization. Practical implication for this repository: check publication permission before adding task statements, provided datasets, flags, or other organizer materials, even if your solution code is yours.
4. **Security activity needs express authorization (§2).** Unauthorized access, exploitation, score manipulation, interference, and obtaining results through unauthorized methods are prohibited. The presence of flags or security-style tasks is not blanket permission to probe the platform. If you encounter a vulnerability, seek an authorized reporting route before testing further.
5. **Violations can affect the whole team (§9).** Sanctions include warnings, restricted access, removed submissions, account suspension, and individual or team disqualification. The terms say seriousness and circumstances will be considered, but do not specify a detailed appeal process.
6. **Account activity is your responsibility (§3).** Protect credentials, provide accurate registration information, and promptly report suspected unauthorized access or misuse. Avoid sharing your login.
7. **You may be responsible for negligent damage as well as intentional damage (§4).** The clause covers material damage to venue property, equipment, infrastructure, and other supplied resources, subject to applicable law.
8. **Platform availability and data preservation are not guaranteed (§5).** The platform is supplied “as is” and “as available.” The liability limitation covers specified consequences of circumstances beyond the organizer's reasonable control, to the extent permitted by law. Practical implication: keep permitted backups of your own work and evidence of submissions; do not depend on uninterrupted access near a deadline.
9. **Media consent is separate and optional (§7).** Where consent is the legal basis, promotional use of identifiable photos and videos requires separate, voluntary, affirmative consent. That consent is not required to participate and can be withdrawn through the Privacy Notice contact details. Withdrawal does not undo prior lawful processing. The clause also says recording may take place; it does not promise that declining consent prevents every recording or every use under another legal basis.
10. **The Privacy Notice supplies essential missing details (§8).** The terms do not give data categories, purposes, legal grounds, recipients, retention periods, or the full rights information. Registration acknowledges access to that notice; it is not consent to processing that separately requires consent.
11. **Organizers interpret the rules and may update the terms (§10).** Material changes must be communicated through official channels. Moldova's law governs. Save the version you received and monitor official announcements.

## What this document does not settle

The following are unresolved in the supplied terms, rather than automatically allowed or prohibited:

- AI assistants, LLMs, coding agents, pretrained models, external datasets, internet access, and external compute.
- Team size, collaboration outside the team, sharing code between teams, and mentor assistance.
- Scoring, evaluation, submission limits, deadlines, tie-breaks, and any prizes or eligibility conditions.
- When solutions may be published and which organizer-provided materials may accompany them.
- The authorized scope for any security testing and the vulnerability-reporting process.
- The specific complaint or appeal procedure and timelines.
- Full personal-data handling details and media consent withdrawal contacts.

These points need the official competition rules, Privacy Notice, or clarification from organizers. Silence in these terms is not evidence of permission.
